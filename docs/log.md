- **2026-10-08 — data: self-play-only weights are unusable, and a sanity test is what caught it** — the obvious follow-up to 2026-10-06 was to put the self-play-tuned weights in a binary and measure them. Held-out loss on the Zurichess tail is **0.058921 vs 0.057992** for the pure-Zurichess fit — i.e. *slightly worse*, exactly as expected when 12% of training becomes out-of-distribution, and exactly why this cannot be settled by loss. SPRT combined-weights vs Zurichess-weights (same code, weights the only difference) is running. **Shipped weights deliberately stay on the Zurichess fit** until that verdict lands; the candidate lives in `bench/engines/torte-combdata`, so HEAD does not drift onto an unmeasured change. - **2026-10-06 — data: train on our own games, and *resolve* tactical positions instead of discarding them** — the 2026-10-06 retune showed king danger learning nothing from Zurichess quiet-labeled, because that set is built by **discarding** any position with a capture or check available — the attacking middlegames the term exists to score. Fix is a second dataset from our own 5,002 games (`bench/results/*.pgn`): `bench/extract-positions.py` samples every 4th ply (consecutive plies are near-duplicates), skips 8 plies of book and the last 4, dedupes on placement, and writes **in game order**; then the new `torte resolve` walks each position forward through winning captures (highest SEE > 0, ≤12 plies) so the position's *static* eval becomes meaningful while the attack itself survives. 109,376 extracted → **96,081 resolved**, of which **23,653 (24.6%) were moved by resolution** — that fraction is roughly the slice Zurichess throws away. All 96,081 emitted FENs re-parse under python-chess, which is the oracle check the hand-written FEN writer needed. **The A/B the whole exercise was for**, same positions, same held-out games, with and without the term: held-out loss **0.150679 without king danger vs 0.150326 with** — an improvement of **0.00035 against 0.00001 on Zurichess**, ~35× more signal, which is what the hypothesis predicted. **Two caveats, neither resolved.** Significance is *not* established: 9,609 held-out positions give an unpaired SE near 0.003, so 0.00035 sits inside it; the comparison is paired (identical positions, near-identical models) which shrinks the true error bar considerably, but quantifying that needs per-position losses. And the fit now **overfits** — train 0.1367 vs held-out 0.1507, a gap of 0.014 — unsurprising with 864 weights on 86k positions where there were 725k, which also means the 2026-09-15 conclusion that *no* L2 ever helps was established on 8× more data and should be re-tested here. **Method fix, applies retroactively:** the tuner held out *every tenth position*, but positions within a game are near-duplicates, so that split straddled the boundary with a game's own positions and the held-out number partly measured memorisation. It now holds out a contiguous tail, which on a game-ordered file means whole games. Earlier held-out figures were therefore mildly optimistic; both configurations shared the flaw, so comparisons between them stand. Shipped weights are deliberately **unchanged** — they still match the binary queued for SPRT, so the term gets measured as one change, not two. (145 tests.)
- **2026-10-06 — eval: king danger retuned — the curve came out convex as predicted, but the loss says the *dataset* is wrong for this term** — 1500 epochs over 652k train / 72.5k held-out, now **864 weights** (the 16 buckets added 32). Two results that disagree, and the disagreement is the finding. **(1) The fitted curve is convex, which is what the term exists to express.** Middlegame weights by attack-unit bucket 0..15: `-14, 0, -15, -18, 5, -18, 21, 9, 57, 18, 19, 91, 102, 104, 185, 152` — noise around zero for light pressure, then a steep climb from bucket 8 onward. A linear term cannot represent that shape at any weight, so the design hypothesis is supported on its own terms. **(2) Held-out loss did not move: 0.057992 vs 0.05800** for the previous (linear `KingAttack`) configuration — a difference of 0.00001, i.e. nothing. The extra freedom redistributed signal rather than adding any. **The likely reason, and it matters beyond this term:** we tune on Zurichess *quiet-labeled* positions, which are filtered to exclude positions with captures or checks available — precisely the positions where king attacks decide games. **We are fitting a king-attack term on a dataset built to exclude king attacks.** That is a structural handicap no amount of epochs fixes, and it applies to any future tactical/threat term too; the fix is training data that includes attacking middlegames (our own game PGNs labelled by result, or a less aggressively filtered set). Also note the endgame column goes *negative* at high buckets (−26, −21, −46), which is weakly determined rather than meaningful: heavy king pressure with few pieces left is rare in quiet positions. **SPRT queued, not yet run** — the machine is at load 13 from three other projects, and the bias here is not symmetric: the king-danger build needs more nodes per position (1,782,979 vs 1,628,363 at bench 8), so starvation penalises the thing under test. - **2026-10-05 — eval: non-linear king danger, with the curve learned rather than written (UNMEASURED)** — `king_attack` is *linear* in attacked king-zone squares, and king safety is not: two attackers is a nuisance, four is usually winning. A linear term cannot express that at any weight, which is why a `≥2 attackers` gate had been bolted onto it. `king_danger` instead sums **attack units** on the enemy king zone (canonical per-attacker weights N=2, B=2, R=3, Q=5 × squares hit), buckets the total into 16, and charges one tunable `[mg, eg]` weight per bucket — so the **shape of the danger curve is fitted from the 725k labelled positions** instead of guessed, and the gate becomes unnecessary (low buckets learn to be worth ≈0). The inner unit weights stay fixed deliberately: letting the tuner scale both the units and the curve is the same degree of freedom twice. New toggle `KingDanger`, and **`KingAttack` now defaults off** — they are alternatives, not companions, and leaving both on would hand the tuner two collinear terms, which is exactly what inverted `Development`'s sign. For the same reason `tune.rs` now fits `SearchConfig::default().eval_config()` rather than `EvalConfig::all()`: the tuner should fit the configuration we actually ship, not a superset containing both alternatives. Seeded with a hand-written escalating curve so the term is sane before tuning (+35 cp for Ng5+Qh5 against a castled king); bench depth 8 moves 1,628,363 → 1,389,394 nodes, which is *expected* here — unlike the movegen work this changes the eval, so the tree legitimately differs. Four material-arithmetic tests now switch `king_danger` off alongside the positional terms they already disabled. (145 tests.) **Nothing is measured yet**: retune then SPRT against the `KingAttack` build, queued behind the lazy-legality match. Treat HEAD as a candidate until that lands.

- **2026-10-04 — search: the abort interval was a node budget pretending to be a time budget (2048 → 256 nodes)** — `MidSearchAbort` consulted the deadline every 2048 nodes, so the wall-clock gap between checks was whatever the machine made of 2048 nodes. When nps collapsed during a contended run it produced **25-29 second overruns and six simultaneous forfeits** at only ~1.6× oversubscription. Interval is now **256**. Justified as correctness, not strength: a forfeit is a lost game regardless of how well the engine was playing. Measurements: `torte bench 8` **1,628,363 nodes, identical** (bench sets no deadline, so the abort path is inert there — the invariant that nothing else changed); **no measurable overhead**, four paired alternating bench rounds giving ratios 0.817/1.270/1.187/0.827, median ≈1.0, i.e. noise in both directions, matching the arithmetic (~4k clock reads/sec at a million nps × tens of ns ≈ 0.01%); and deadline adherence at `go movetime 300` improved from 217-298 ms reported to **167-184 ms**, both inside budget but the smaller interval stopping more promptly. **What this does not do:** checking more often narrows the worst case eightfold, it cannot remove it — the overshoot is still unbounded in principle, because only a clock read can bound a clock. The honest fix is to keep the interval small rather than to pick a clever N. The pathological case (deliberate CPU starvation) was **not** tested, because generating that load would have contaminated the Stockfish calibration match running at the time. Side observation worth having on record: engine **startup costs 0.42 s**, all of it the magic-bitboard search — that is why `movetime 300` measures ~0.9 s of wall time end to end, and it is not a clock problem.
- **2026-10-04 — bench: delta pruning and the aspiration width are both worth nothing (and a forfeit mechanism worth fixing)** — the two toggles that had shipped without ever being measured alone, each same-binary with one `setoption` differing. **`DeltaPruning` off vs on: +2 ± 31 Elo** over 350 games (136W/134L/80D). **`AspirationDelta` 400 vs 100: +2 ± 31** over 350 games (132W/130L/88D). Both dead level, so both keep their current setting on the *node* argument alone: delta pruning stays on (−13% nodes for free) and the window stays at 100 (−15% nodes for free), neither buying or costing strength. Taken with the LMP result, our search is insensitive to these knobs at this strength — which is the 2026-09-15 diagnosis holding up: the gap is eval. **Separately, a robustness finding from the same logs:** the aspiration run recorded 15 time forfeits, six of them *simultaneous* 25-29 second overruns, at a load that was only ~1.6× oversubscribed — too small to explain a 100× slowdown by starvation alone. The mechanism is that `MidSearchAbort` consults the deadline every **2048 nodes**, so a node-count budget is not a time budget: when nps collapses (contention, a swap stall), the interval between checks stretches without bound and the engine blows the clock. A real tournament machine makes this unlikely but not impossible, and it is cheap to fix — check elapsed time every N nodes *and* cap N by a wall-clock interval, or simply drop the interval sharply. Logged rather than fixed, because an unmeasured search change is exactly what this fortnight has been paying for.
- **2026-10-04 — movegen: lazy legality in the search (option A of the copy-per-move fix)** — `generate_legal_moves` proved every pseudo-legal move legal by copying the 120-byte `Board`, applying the move and testing king safety — **~35 copies per node to serve the 1-3 moves a node actually searches** once beta cutoffs are counted. `negamax_inner` and `qsearch` now call the new `generate_pseudo_legal_moves` and test legality *lazily*, on the board the search has to make anyway (`own_king_safe`, one attack test, no extra copy). - **2026-10-04 — board: the incremental Zobrist hash was already in (since 2026-05-11); the docs were stale, and the update now skips the legality filter** — four places (`CLAUDE.md`, `docs/future-work.md`, `docs/architecture.md`, roadmap item 5) still said the hash is recomputed from scratch at every node. It is not: `apply_move` has XOR-updated `board.zobrist` since `9b465c7` and the search reads it directly. What this entry adds is **proof and one real optimisation**. Proof: a perft-style oracle test walks six trees (startpos d4, kiwipete d3, perft positions 3-5, and a bare-rooks position for rook-takes-rook on a home square) — ~470k nodes — asserting `board.zobrist == zobrist_hash(board)` at *every* node, so castling both ways for both colours, en passant, promotions including capture-promotions, rights revoked by capture, and ep squares set and cleared are all covered. Negative control: deleting any one of the four deltas (castling-rights mask, ep file, ep-captured pawn, castling rook) makes it fail. Optimisation: `generate_legal_moves` tests king safety by applying every pseudo-legal move to a throwaway copy — ~35 per node — and each of those copies was paying the full incremental update for a key nobody ever reads. `apply_move_unhashed` (a `const HASH: bool` variant of the same body) skips it there; the search path still hashes. Also `Board::new()` left `zobrist` at 0, which is not the hash of an empty board (the castling-rights term is non-zero), so it is now seeded exactly like `parse`. **The measurement is a negative result.** `bench 8` nodes are identical — **1,628,363** — across master, this change, and a build with incremental hashing removed entirely, which is the correctness result worth having (same keys, same tree). Wall clock cannot separate the three on this machine, with chess matches holding load at 7-19: 20 paired rounds (the three builds run back to back each round) give median within-round ratios **1.012** for this change vs master, **0.993** for no-incremental-hashing vs master and **1.018** for this change vs no-incremental, against a per-build spread of 1.5-5.3 s; best-of-10 knps reads master 654 / this change 610 / no-incremental 565, an ordering that *contradicts* the paired medians. So: no speedup demonstrated, and **removing incremental hashing altogether also costs nothing measurable** — the roadmap's "probably ~20% speedup" is not there to find. The structural reason is worth keeping: a from-scratch hash runs once or twice per node, while the incremental update runs ~35 times per node inside the legality filter, so the two strategies cost within a few percent of each other. The real win in this area is a movegen that does not copy a board per move (pin-aware legal generation), not a cheaper hash. Re-measure on a quiet machine before trusting any timing here. (2 new tests, 144 total.)
- **2026-10-04 — bench: no LMP threshold wins, and the reason is structural** — three settings played against the LMP-off default (same binary, UCI spins): base 5 = 6/10/16 (the original) **−132 ± 38** over 284 games; base 12 = 13/16/21 **−113 ± 39** over 300 games, LLR −2.85 (all but accepted H0); base 20 depth≤2 = 21/24 **−36 ± 75** over 67 games (partial, machine reclaimed). Monotone improvement as the budget grows, and **never positive** — which is exactly what the shape predicts: a bigger budget prunes less, so the setting converges on *doing nothing* and the Elo approaches 0 from below. **There is no threshold that makes this LMP a gain; the tuning question is closed.** This confirms the prediction logged on 09-30 before the runs: the fault was never the numbers. Strong engines use *more* aggressive budgets (`3 + depth²` = 4/7/12) than the 6/10/16 that lost 132 Elo here, so what they have and we lack are the **guards** that make count-based pruning safe — they do not prune moves that give check, nor killer/high-history moves, and they only prune in non-PV nodes. Ours prunes any quiet move past a count. Next experiment is therefore guards at the original thresholds, not more threshold search; `LmpBase`/`LmpMaxDepth` stay exposed because they are the knob the guards will need re-tuned against.
